# Speaker Notes by Slide

## Slide 1: Title (30 seconds)

"Good morning. Today I'll present our analysis of NASCAR sponsorship visibility. We set out to
answer a simple question: which sponsors deliver the best exposure for the investment? Let me
walk you through what we found."

---

## Slide 2: Executive Summary (1 minute)

"Let me start with the bottom line before we dive into the details. We analyzed four primary
sponsors across all 36 races of the 2024 season. The data tells us three things: NAPA delivers
the most raw exposure, Love's delivers the best value per dollar, and FedEx is the only sponsor
that ranks near the top on both. Based on that, our primary recommendation is FedEx. Now let me
show you how we got there."

---

## Slide 3: Project Approach (1.5 minutes)

"To build a fair comparison, we needed a consistent methodology. We gathered data from three
sources: race results from public databases, social engagement from Reddit and YouTube, and news
coverage from manual tracking. We then built a scoring model that weights these factors by their
importance for sponsor visibility. Race performance gets 40% because wins and strong finishes put
the car on camera; media coverage 30%; social 20%; and special events like wins and playoffs 10%."

---

## Slide 4: Data and Limitations (1 minute)

"Before I show you the findings, let me be transparent about what we could and couldn't measure.
Most importantly: sponsorship costs are estimates, not actual contract values. The industry does
not publish these figures, so we used industry publications and comparable analysis. There is
uncertainty here, roughly 15 to 20 percent. With that caveat stated, let me show you what we found."

---

## Slide 5: Finding #1 (1.5 minutes)

"Our first key finding is the most surprising. Look at this efficiency chart."
[Point to visualization]
"NAPA has the highest total visibility, but it ranks LAST in value per dollar. Love's, which is
last in raw exposure, is FIRST in efficiency at 82 points per million. The strategic implication
for NY Racing is that the most-seen sponsor is not automatically the smartest spend."

---

## Slide 6: Finding #2 (1.5 minutes)

"The second pattern is about balance."
[Point to visualization]
"FedEx sits at number two on both dimensions. It captures almost all of the top sponsor's exposure
at a much better value. No other sponsor is strong on both. This is why FedEx becomes our
recommendation: it minimizes the trade-off you'd otherwise have to make."

---

## Slide 7: Finding #3 (1.5 minutes)

"Finally, and perhaps most importantly for your confidence in this: the recommendation is robust."
[Point to visualization]
"We re-ran the model under four different weighting schemes. The ranking barely moved, at most one
position. This changes how we should think about the result: it is not an artifact of our specific
weights, it is a stable pattern in the data."

---

## Slide 8: Recommendations (2 minutes)

"So what should NY Racing do with this information? Our primary recommendation is FedEx. Here's why:
it is the only sponsor top-two on both visibility and efficiency, it had the most wins, and it
delivers near-max exposure at a better price than the top-visibility option. For teams with
different priorities, Love's offers the best pure value, and NAPA offers the most exposure if budget
is no object."

---

## Slide 9: Trade-offs (1.5 minutes)

"Ultimately, this is a strategic choice, not a math problem. Let me show you the trade-offs."
[Walk through scenario comparison]
"The question for NY Racing is: what matters more, maximum visibility or maximum efficiency per
dollar? If it's exposure, NAPA. If it's value, Love's. If it's balance, FedEx."

---

## Slide 10: Next Steps (1 minute)

"Before we open for questions, let me suggest next steps. First, validate the cost estimates with
direct conversations, since that is our biggest source of uncertainty. Second, consider analyzing
additional seasons to see if the patterns hold. Third, assess brand fit and activation value that
this analysis does not capture. I'm happy to answer questions. What would you like to discuss?"
