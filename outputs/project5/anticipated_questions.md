# Anticipated Questions and Prepared Answers

## Technical Questions

### Q: "How did you determine the weights (40/30/20/10)?"

**Answer**: "The weights come from industry sponsorship-ROI frameworks and were validated by our
sensitivity analysis. We started with published category ranges (performance 30-40%, media 25-35%,
social 20-30%), then tested alternative configurations. The rankings are stable across reasonable
weight variations."

**Supporting evidence**: sensitivity analysis chart, methodology document.

---

### Q: "How reliable are the cost estimates?"

**Answer**: "These are estimates, not actual contract values. We used Forbes, Sports Business
Journal, and industry benchmarks. There's roughly 15 to 20 percent uncertainty. However, our
efficiency rankings remain stable even when we test with high and low cost estimates."

**Supporting evidence**: cost sensitivity analysis.

---

### Q: "Why only four sponsors?"

**Answer**: "We selected sponsors with full-season primary presence and sufficient data across all
channels. Four gave us a meaningful comparison across different team tiers while keeping the analysis
rigorous. The methodology scales to more sponsors with additional data collection time."

---

### Q: "Which sponsor should we pursue?"

**Answer**: "FedEx offers the best combination of visibility and efficiency. However, if your
priority is maximum exposure, NAPA is stronger; if it's pure value, Love's wins. I'd recommend
confirming the actual FedEx deal cost before finalizing."

---

### Q: "What about sponsors you didn't analyze?"

**Answer**: "Our methodology can be applied to any sponsor with available data. If there's a
specific sponsor you're interested in, we can run the same analysis. The framework is the real
deliverable here; these specific rankings are just one application."

---

### Q: "How confident are you in these recommendations?"

**Answer**: "I'm confident in the methodology and the relative rankings. The model is robust to
weight changes and cost-estimate variations. The main uncertainty is in the absolute cost
estimates, which I recommend validating with direct conversations before any major decision."

## Tough Questions

### Q: "This seems like a lot of work for obvious conclusions."

**Answer**: "The value isn't just the ranking, it's the ability to quantify and compare. Before
this analysis, we had intuitions about sponsor value. Now we can put numbers on those intuitions
and make defensible decisions. The methodology also gives us a framework for evaluating future
opportunities."

---

### Q: "What if the 2024 season isn't representative?"

**Answer**: "That's a real risk. This analysis is based on one season. I'd recommend validating
with two to three additional seasons before major commitments. The good news is the methodology
scales; we can run this for multiple years with the same approach."

---

### Q: "Why should we trust social media data?"

**Answer**: "Social media is a proxy, not a perfect measure. But Reddit's r/NASCAR is the largest
online fan community, and YouTube engagement reflects post-race consumption. These platforms
capture active fan engagement that traditional TV ratings miss. We weighted social at 20%, not 50%,
acknowledging its limitations. And I'll be transparent: in our data, Reddit and total YouTube were
race-level, so news coverage carries most of the sponsor-level signal."
